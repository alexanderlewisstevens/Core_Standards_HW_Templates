# Proofs Core — instructor notes

Private production and grading guidance. Do not publish or place in a
student-visible Canvas area.

## Live-form structure

- 20 minutes, supervised, on paper.
- Five labeled problems total: select one complete problem from each of A--E.
- Each selected label is one scoring unit, including all required parts.
- Use 0 = Not Yet, 4 = Meets, 5 = Exceeds in Canvas.
- Meets: four of five labeled problems are fully correct.
- Exceeds: all five are fully correct.
- A retake replaces the entire five-problem form, including a retake from Meets
  to Exceeds.
- A student may attempt this Core once per calendar day. A second, different
  Core on the same day is permitted only after the first is passed.

## Section roles

| Section | Essential target | Typical time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Execute or diagnose a short propositional/predicate inference and cite rules | 2–3 minutes |
| B | Construct a concise direct proof from definitions | 3–4 minutes |
| C | Construct a proof by contraposition or contradiction | 4–5 minutes |
| D | Use cases, a constructive witness, or a counterexample appropriately | 3–4 minutes |
| E | Select a defensible strategy or repair a flawed proof | 3–4 minutes |

The 16–20 minute aggregate estimate leaves a small review margin. Time every
assembled combination before use; do not pair unusually long variants across
multiple sections.

## Fully-correct standard

A labeled problem is fully correct only when it:

1. states the relevant hypotheses or starts with an arbitrary required object;
2. uses definitions with correctly scoped witnesses and variables;
3. justifies the central inference or algebraic step;
4. reaches the exact requested conclusion; and
5. completes every requested subpart.

Minor notation that does not alter the reasoning can receive feedback without
making the problem incorrect. A missing logical direction, unjustified division,
reused existential witness, assumed conclusion, or example offered in place of a
universal proof is an essential error.

## Instructional alignment

- Week 4 Sets and Functions is prerequisite language, so B.5 and E.5 deliberately
  use element arguments after that material has been taught.
- Tuesday group work should sort statements by promising method, complete one
  full proof, and critique/repair another group's proof over 30–50 minutes.
- Thursday's second half is the collaborative Group Core Proofs Attempt. The
  individual supervised Core opens at the section midpoint but is administered
  separately; it is not part of the group packet and does not need to occur in
  that same class meeting.
- The LaTeX Formal Solutions assignment should ask students to typeset a revised
  proof after mathematical feedback, emphasizing structure and readable notation
  rather than LaTeX tricks.

## Grader handoff: minimum evidence and alternatives

- **A:** the conclusion follows by valid propositional or quantified inference,
  with the central rules named and existential witnesses kept distinct unless
  equality is proved. Any valid derivation or genuine countermodel is
  acceptable.
- **B:** a direct proof starts from arbitrary objects satisfying the hypotheses,
  expands the relevant definitions, supplies required integer or set witnesses,
  and reaches the exact conclusion. Algebraically equivalent witnesses are
  acceptable.
- **C:** contraposition or contradiction must be announced or unmistakable,
  assume the logically correct statement, and produce the requested conclusion
  or a genuine contradiction. A different valid indirect method is acceptable
  unless the prompt names the method.
- **D:** cases must be exhaustive; constructive existence needs an explicit
  witness; refutation needs a counterexample satisfying every hypothesis. Any
  valid partition, witness, or counterexample is acceptable.
- **E:** the response identifies a defensible method or the first essential
  flaw and supplies a repair that removes it without assuming the conclusion.
  Different correct repairs are acceptable.

Use the shortest argument that demonstrates every essential target; do not
require a particular prose style or the same intermediate algebra as the key.
Do not carry section credit across attempts: each retake is a complete new A--E
form scored again as `0`, `4`, or `5`.

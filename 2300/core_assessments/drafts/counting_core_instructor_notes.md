# Counting Core — Instructor Notes

> Private planning document. Do not publish or link for students.

## Core format and grading

- A supervised attempt lasts 20 minutes and contains exactly five problems, one selected from each A–E section.
- A: fundamental counting rules; B: arrangements and selections; C: constrained counting; D: probability models; E: modeling and interpretation.
- Each labeled problem is all-or-nothing. Every requested calculation, model, and short justification must be correct for the problem to count.
- Enter `0` for Not Yet, `4` for Meets (four of five fully correct), or `5` for Exceeds (all five fully correct).
- A retake replaces the entire five-problem Core, including after Meets. One attempt per Core per day; a second, different Core on the same day is allowed only after the first is passed.
- The Counting Core closes at the final-exam deadline.
- Because E.2, E.3, and E.5 are the longest prompts, do not combine more than one of them with unusually long selections from the other sections. Verify every assembled A–E form against the 20-minute limit before printing.

## Source alignment

- A uses Rosen 7e §§6.1–6.2 problem families: sum/product rules, functions, inclusion–exclusion, and generalized pigeonhole reasoning.
- B uses §§6.3–6.4: combinations, permutations, repeated objects, fixed-weight bit strings, and the binomial theorem.
- C combines the standard constrained-counting models in §§6.1, 6.3, and 6.4.
- D uses §§7.1–7.2: equally likely outcomes, probability identities, combinations in probability, sampling without replacement, and conditional probability.
- E uses §§7.2–7.4: independence, total probability, Bayes' theorem, expected value, and aggregation/base-rate interpretation.
- Exact exercise numbers should remain private until checked against the assigned edition. The retired breakfast variants and additional aggregation tables may be kept as optional practice, but they are not live Core categories.

- About 60 minutes: sum/product rules, functions and injective functions, pigeonhole reasoning, permutations versus combinations, and binomial coefficients.
- Final 45–50 minutes: `Week 8 Workshop — Counting Core Preparation`. Use substantial A–C tasks and require the group to name and justify each model before calculating.

### Thursday, October 29

- About 60 minutes: constrained counting, complements and case splits, and comparison of two valid counting models.
- Final 45–50 minutes: `Op-Ed — Peer Review`. Counting practice may supply lecture examples but must not become a second graded activity.

## Week 9 teaching plan

### Tuesday, November 3

- About 60 minutes: sample spaces, events, complements, conditional probability, independence, combinations in probability, and aggregation/base-rate effects.
- Final 45–50 minutes: `Week 9 Workshop — Probability Core Preparation`, restricted to D–E practice and available only at the class midpoint.

- The separate individual Counting Core opens at the midpoint for supervised attempts.

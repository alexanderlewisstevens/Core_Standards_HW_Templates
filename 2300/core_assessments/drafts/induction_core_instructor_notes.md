# Induction Core — Instructor Notes

> Private planning document. Do not publish or link for students.

## Selected Core format

- `induction_assessment.tex` is the authoritative two-page student form. It has
  exactly three scored problems: A.1 (short strong-induction setup), B.1 (short
  diagnosis and repair), and C.1 (one complete inductive proof).
- C.1 supplies Pascal's identity and asks students to prove the binomial-sum
  identity. Students are not asked to prove Pascal's identity within the
  20-minute Core.
- `drafts/induction_extended_problem_bank.tex` preserves the former A--E bank
  privately for practice, workshops, and equivalent retake development. Do
  not publish the bank as the live Core.
- `induction_assessment_long_proof.tex` preserves the earlier version that asks
  students first to prove Pascal's identity and then prove the binomial-sum
  identity. Retain it privately as a typed-proof or group-work candidate.

## Core format and grading

- The live Core is a supervised, 20-minute paper assessment containing exactly
  three problems: A.1, B.1, and C.1.
- Each labeled problem is all-or-nothing; every requested part must be correct
  for that problem to count.
- C.1 is essential. Record the result in Canvas as:
  - `0` (below Meets): C.1 is not fully correct, or neither A.1 nor B.1 is fully correct;
  - `4` (Meets): C.1 and exactly one of A.1 or B.1 are fully correct;
  - `5` (Exceeds): A.1, B.1, and C.1 are all fully correct.
- A retake always replaces the entire three-problem form, including after a student has earned Meets.
- A student may attempt a given Core at most once per day. A second, different Core may be attempted on the same day only after the first Core attempted that day is passed.
- The Induction Core closes at the final-exam deadline. The final is the last opportunity to pass or improve it.
- Before printing an equivalent retake form, verify that A and B remain short,
  that C contains the only lengthy proof, and that all necessary identities are
  supplied. Target roughly 3 minutes for A, 4 minutes for B, and 12--13 minutes
  for C.

## Source alignment

The Core is aligned primarily with Rosen, *Discrete Mathematics and Its
Applications*, Sections 5.1--5.2:

- A.1 tests the architecture of strong induction through prime factorization.
- B.1 tests whether students can recognize circular reasoning and repair an
  ordinary-induction step.
- C.1 tests construction of a complete ordinary-induction proof, including the
  reindexing needed to use Pascal's identity.
- Structural induction and recursively defined objects remain teaching and
  practice material but are not independently certified by this shorter Core.

Keep exact exercise-number claims out of student-facing material unless they have been checked against the edition assigned in Canvas.

## Week 7 teaching plan

### Tuesday, October 20

- About 60 minutes: identify the proposition `P(n)`; establish the base case; state the inductive hypothesis precisely; show exactly where it is used; compare ordinary and strong induction; diagnose a circular or incomplete step.
- Final 45–50 minutes: `Week 7 Workshop — Induction Core Preparation`. Practice
  one setup problem, one diagnosis problem, and one complete proof. This is
  group practice, not an attendance proxy.

### Thursday, October 22

- About 60 minutes: strong induction, recursive objects, structural induction, and the Pascal-identity step in a binomial-sum proof.
- Final 45–50 minutes: `Group Core Induction Attempt`. Give each group one
  three-part practice form; every member handwrites at least one complete
  solution.
- The separate individual Induction Core opens at the midpoint of class for supervised attempts. Do not make either activity available before the midpoint.

# Arguments Core — Instructor Notes

Keep this file private. It contains form-construction rules and answer keys; it
is not student-facing material.

## Live-form specification

- Administer each attempt on paper under supervision for **20 minutes**.
- Build every live form with **five labeled problems**, one from each section
  A–E. Sections B–D are therefore three of the five problems and the majority of
  the form.
- Each labeled problem is one scoring unit, including all of its required
  subparts. A partially correct multi-part problem does not count as correct.
- Record one holistic Canvas result: **0 = Not Yet, 4 = Meets, 5 = Exceeds**.
  Meets means at least four of five problems are fully correct; Exceeds means all
  five are fully correct.
- A retake replaces the entire form. Students do not retake only the missed
  problem or section.
- A student may attempt one Core per day. A second Core on the same day is
  permitted only after the first Core is passed.
- Live forms use new scenarios or instructor-authored adaptations from the same
  problem families. Do not copy or closely paraphrase official LSAC questions,
  stimuli, answer choices, or explanations.

## Timing and selection

The public Core is a practice bank, not a single live form. Do not place the
longest bank prompts on a 20-minute form without shortening them.

- A: use one reconstruction, conditional-classification, or validity task.
  If adapting A.3, use one or at most two of its four arguments, not all four.
- B–D: use one concise Logical Reasoning problem from each section. Require both
  the selected answer and a brief justification.
- E: use a focused evaluation or revision task. If adapting E.3, ask no more
  than two tightly related subparts on one live form.
- Target roughly 3–4 minutes for each B–D problem and 4–5 minutes for each A/E
  problem. Pilot every form before use; revise wording if a prepared student
  cannot finish comfortably in 20 minutes.

  keys and must remain unpublished.

## Provenance

Sections B–D are original LSAT-style analogues inspired by the problem-type
distribution in LSAC's publicly disclosed January 2023, February 2024, and April
2025 tests. They are not endorsed by LSAC. Keep official practice in LawHub and
keep public course materials limited to original course-authored text.

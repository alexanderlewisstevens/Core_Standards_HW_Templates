% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q1 | counterargument
\item A town is considering replacing diesel library vans with electric vans.
An opponent objects that the electric vans cost more to purchase. Which response,
if true, most strongly answers that objection?
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item The town recently repainted the existing vans.
  \item Some residents prefer printed books to electronic books.
  \item The electric vans can carry fewer books per trip.
  \item Fuel and maintenance savings over the vans' service lives exceed the
        difference in purchase price.
  \item A neighboring town operates no mobile library.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q2 | argument completion
\item A museum director argues that the museum should keep evening hours on
Thursdays. Visitor counts are currently low on weekday evenings, but the only
weekday evening now offered is Monday, when most nearby restaurants are closed.
The director's argument is most logically completed by which statement?
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item Restaurant hours never affect museum attendance.
  \item Thursday is the least convenient evening for museum employees.
  \item Low Monday attendance does not show that Thursday evening attendance
        would also be low.
  \item The museum should eliminate all daytime hours.
  \item Every Thursday visitor would also buy dinner nearby.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q3 | point at issue
\item Nara says, ``The city should permit food carts in the central plaza because
they would make lunch less expensive.'' Ivo replies, ``The plaza's narrow paths
would become dangerously crowded, so food carts should not be permitted there.''
Nara and Ivo disagree about whether
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item food carts generally sell less expensive lunches than restaurants do.
  \item the city should allow food carts in the central plaza.
  \item the plaza has narrower paths than other city spaces.
  \item crowded places are always dangerous.
  \item the city regulates restaurant prices.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q4 | strengthen EXCEPT
\item A biologist concludes that a newly observed decline in pond frogs was
caused by a pesticide used on nearby lawns. Each of the following, if true,
strengthens the conclusion \emph{except}:
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item The decline began soon after local use of the pesticide increased.
  \item Laboratory exposure to the pesticide reduces frog survival.
  \item Comparable ponds without pesticide runoff show no decline.
  \item The ponds contain several native species of aquatic plants.
  \item Concentrations of the pesticide are highest in ponds with the largest
        frog declines.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q5 | most strongly supported
\item All ceramic pieces in the east display case were made before 1920. Every
piece made before 1920 in that case is insured. At least one blue ceramic piece
is in the east display case. Which statement is most strongly supported?
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item Every insured piece is ceramic.
  \item At least one blue ceramic piece is insured.
  \item No piece made after 1920 is insured.
  \item Every blue object in the museum is ceramic.
  \item Some insured pieces were made after 1920.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q6 | role of statement
\item A gardener argues: ``The courtyard should be planted with thyme rather
than turf. Thyme needs less water than turf, and water use must be reduced this
summer. Moreover, thyme tolerates foot traffic.'' In the argument, the statement
that water use must be reduced this summer serves as
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item the conclusion the gardener seeks to establish.
  \item background that is irrelevant to the recommendation.
  \item a principle that, with another premise, supports the recommendation.
  \item a claim offered to refute the fact that thyme tolerates foot traffic.
  \item an example of a planting plan already adopted.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q7 | must be true
\item Every archivist on the digitization team reads Latin or Greek. No team
member who reads Greek works only mornings. Mira is an archivist on the team and
works only mornings. Which statement must be true?
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item Mira reads Latin.
  \item Mira reads both Latin and Greek.
  \item Mira supervises the team.
  \item No archivist reads Greek.
  \item Every morning worker reads Latin.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q8 | strengthen EXCEPT
\item A transit planner predicts that adding secure bicycle lockers at the train
station will increase commuter rail use. Each of the following strengthens the
prediction \emph{except}:
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item Many nearby residents say fear of bicycle theft keeps them from riding
        to the station.
  \item Similar stations gained riders after installing secure lockers.
  \item The lockers will be placed next to the station entrance.
  \item The rail line's weekend timetable will remain unchanged.
  \item Most surveyed cyclists consider the proposed rental fee affordable.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q9 | role of statement
\item A critic writes: ``Some claim that the novel's fragmented chronology makes
it incoherent. Yet each time shift is announced by a change in narrator, and the
sequence of narrators follows a repeated pattern. Thus, the chronology is
demanding but intelligible.'' The statement about the repeated pattern serves to
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item state the objection the critic ultimately accepts.
  \item provide evidence for the critic's conclusion.
  \item identify a consequence of the novel's incoherence.
  \item concede that the time shifts are accidental.
  \item define what all novels must accomplish.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q10 | resolve discrepancy EXCEPT
\item After a cafeteria introduced smaller plates, diners left less food uneaten,
yet the cafeteria purchased the same amount of food per week. Each statement
could help explain the result \emph{except}:
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item The cafeteria began serving more diners each week.
  \item Kitchen spoilage increased during the same period.
  \item Diners returned for second servings more often.
  \item The cafeteria donated more unserved food than before.
  \item The cafeteria's food purchases were measured by weight both before and
        after the change.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q11 | necessary assumption
\item A school will replace printed notices with an app to reduce paper waste.
The principal concludes that the change will substantially reduce the school's
total paper use. The conclusion requires assuming that
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item every family owns the newest model of phone.
  \item no teacher will ever print an app message.
  \item the app will cost nothing to maintain.
  \item students prefer notifications to paper notices.
  \item eliminating printed notices will produce a substantial net reduction
        rather than merely shift comparable paper use to other tasks.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q12 | weaken
\item An editor concludes that articles with shorter headlines attract more
readers because, in last month's issue, articles with headlines under six words
received more online views than articles with longer headlines. Which statement
most weakens the conclusion?
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item Some short headlines contain uncommon words.
  \item The magazine publishes twelve issues each year.
  \item The most-viewed articles were promoted prominently on the home page,
        while most articles with longer headlines were not.
  \item Many readers access the magazine on phones.
  \item Editors sometimes revise headlines before publication.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q13 | sufficient assumption
\item Any sculpture selected for the atrium must be weather-resistant and safe
to touch. The committee knows that sculpture $S$ is safe to touch. Which added
assumption allows the committee to conclude that $S$ is eligible for selection?
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item Every weather-resistant sculpture is expensive.
  \item Some eligible sculptures are not safe to touch.
  \item Sculpture $S$ is more colorful than the current atrium sculpture.
  \item Sculpture $S$ is weather-resistant, and anything both weather-resistant
        and safe to touch is eligible for selection.
  \item No indoor sculpture is weather-resistant.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q14 | flaw
\item During the month when a company introduced standing desks, employee sick
days decreased. A manager concludes that the desks caused the decrease. The
reasoning is most vulnerable because it
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item assumes that sick days can never be counted accurately.
  \item infers causation from a temporal association without excluding other
        possible causes.
  \item treats a necessary condition as sufficient.
  \item concludes that every employee used a standing desk.
  \item relies on a claim that contradicts the observed decrease.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q15 | strengthen
\item A historian argues that an unsigned diary was written by a particular
surveyor because the diary uses several unusual abbreviations found in the
surveyor's signed field notes. Which statement most strengthens the argument?
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item The diary describes a region the surveyor never visited.
  \item Several writers in the same office used those abbreviations.
  \item The abbreviations were rare among other writers of the period, and the
        diary's paper dates from the surveyor's working years.
  \item The surveyor's field notes contain maps as well as prose.
  \item The diary was purchased at an auction decades later.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q16 | flaw
\item A dean argues: ``Both the astronomy club and the poetry club want longer
library hours. Therefore, the clubs should be able to agree on a joint proposal
for how the library's new budget should be spent.'' The argument is flawed because
it
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item overlooks that neither club uses the library.
  \item assumes that the library already has longer hours.
  \item treats a disagreement as evidence of agreement.
  \item concludes that the clubs have identical memberships.
  \item presumes that agreement on one goal guarantees agreement on how to pursue
        or fund that goal.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q17 | parallel reasoning
\item Which argument most closely parallels this reasoning? ``Every certified
guide has completed first-aid training. Lian is a certified guide. Therefore,
Lian has completed first-aid training.''
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item Some copper roofs turn green; this roof is green; therefore it is copper.
  \item All reserve trails close at dusk; Cedar Trail is a reserve trail;
        therefore Cedar Trail closes at dusk.
  \item No glass object is flexible; this object is not glass; therefore it is
        flexible.
  \item Every baker measures flour; Ren measures flour; therefore Ren is a baker.
  \item Some poems rhyme; this text is a poem; therefore it rhymes.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q18 | flaw
\item A council member says that widening the riverside path will reduce travel
time for cyclists because wider paths permit faster riding. The argument overlooks
the possibility that
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item some cyclists own more than one bicycle.
  \item travel time is measured in minutes.
  \item the river runs through more than one district.
  \item the widened path will attract enough additional users to create new
        congestion.
  \item path construction requires gravel.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q19 | sufficient assumption
\item Every grant proposal that demonstrates community benefit receives a full
review. Proposal $P$ includes free public workshops. Which assumption is
sufficient to conclude that proposal $P$ will receive a full review?
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item Some proposals receiving full review include workshops.
  \item Proposal $P$ requests less money than proposal $Q$.
  \item Any proposal that includes free public workshops demonstrates community
        benefit.
  \item Only proposals with workshops receive a full review.
  \item Community benefit is difficult to measure.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q20 | necessary assumption
\item A reviewer concludes that a new translation is more accurate than the old
one because it preserves every technical term that the old translation omits.
The conclusion depends on assuming that
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item preserving those terms does not introduce errors that outweigh the
        improvement.
  \item the old translation is shorter.
  \item every reader knows the source language.
  \item technical terms are the only words in the text.
  \item the new translator has published other books.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q21 | apply principle
\item Principle: When a public agency can prevent a serious, foreseeable harm at
modest cost without violating anyone's rights, it should do so. Which action is
most strongly supported by the principle?
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item A library removes an unpopular novel to avoid complaints.
  \item A town buys decorative banners although its bridges need repair.
  \item A school publishes students' medical records to encourage caution.
  \item A park installs an inexpensive railing beside a frequently used path
        next to a concealed drop-off.
  \item A museum closes permanently because one visitor once slipped.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q22 | sufficient assumption
\item A policy analyst argues: ``If the pilot program reduces processing time,
the agency should expand it. The pilot reduced the median processing time by
three days. Therefore, the agency should expand it.'' Which assumption makes the
reasoning valid?
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item The pilot cost more than expected.
  \item Every case was processed exactly three days faster.
  \item No other agency has tried the program.
  \item Expansion will eliminate all delays.
  \item A three-day reduction in the median counts as a reduction in processing
        time for purposes of the policy.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q23 | flaw
\item The regional orchard industry produced more apples this year than last
year. Therefore, each orchard in the region must have produced more apples this
year. The reasoning is flawed because it
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item assumes that apples can be counted.
  \item confuses a prediction with a recommendation.
  \item attributes a change in the whole group to every member of the group.
  \item uses a sample too small to represent one orchard.
  \item concludes that total production decreased.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q24 | parallel flaw
\item Which argument contains reasoning most similar to this flawed argument?
``Anyone admitted to the conservatory must audition. Toma auditioned, so Toma
will be admitted.''
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item All bronze statues are heavy; this statue is bronze; so it is heavy.
  \item Anyone entering the lab must wear goggles; Pia wore goggles; so Pia
        entered the lab.
  \item No reptiles have fur; this animal has fur; so it is not a reptile.
  \item Some volunteers speak Arabic; Zed volunteers; so Zed might speak Arabic.
  \item Every accepted paper is reviewed; this paper was not reviewed; so it was
        not accepted.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q25 | epistemic flaw
\item Researchers have not found evidence that the island once supported bears.
Therefore, bears certainly never lived on the island. The reasoning is flawed
because it
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item assumes that bears can live only on islands.
  \item confuses the past with the future.
  \item relies on evidence that bears currently live there.
  \item treats a lack of discovered evidence as conclusive evidence of absence.
  \item establishes only that every island supported bears.
  \end{enumerate}

% Source map: January 2023 | Logical Reasoning | Q26 | principle support
\item A review board must choose between two preservation projects. Project R
would protect a unique mural at immediate risk; project S would improve storage
for many replaceable display panels. Which principle most supports choosing
project R?
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item Always choose the project that affects the greatest number of objects.
  \item Public projects should never involve visual art.
  \item Storage improvements are always more valuable than restoration.
  \item A project is justified only when it produces revenue.
  \item Give priority to preventing imminent, irreversible loss when competing
        benefits can be obtained later.
  \end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}

\section*{Answer Key and Concise Explanations}

\begin{description}
\item[1. D] Lifetime savings directly answer the higher purchase-price objection.
\item[2. C] The existing Monday evidence does not determine demand on Thursday.
\item[3. B] Their conclusions directly conflict over permitting carts in the plaza.
\item[4. D] Native plants do not connect the pesticide to the frog decline.
\item[5. B] The blue ceramic piece is pre-1920 and therefore insured.
\item[6. C] The water-reduction requirement links lower water use to the recommendation.
\item[7. A] Mira cannot be a Greek-reading morning-only worker, so she reads Latin.
\item[8. D] An unchanged weekend timetable does not support the locker prediction.
\item[9. B] The repeated pattern is evidence that the chronology is intelligible.
\item[10. E] A stable measurement method restates the result but does not explain it.
\item[11. E] A substantial net reduction must result rather than a shift of comparable paper use.
\item[12. C] Unequal promotion offers a strong alternative explanation for the views.
\item[13. D] It supplies weather resistance and a rule sufficient for eligibility.
\item[14. B] Timing alone does not establish that the desks caused the decrease.
\item[15. C] Rarity plus compatible dating makes common authorship more likely.
\item[16. E] Shared support for longer hours does not ensure agreement on allocation.
\item[17. B] Both arguments apply a universal rule to a known member of its class.
\item[18. D] Induced congestion could offset the speed benefit.
\item[19. C] Workshops then establish benefit, which guarantees review.
\item[20. A] The cited improvement cannot establish greater overall accuracy if it creates worse errors.
\item[21. D] The railing prevents a serious foreseeable harm cheaply and without a rights violation.
\item[22. E] It connects the measured median reduction to the policy's condition.
\item[23. C] A higher regional total need not mean that every orchard increased output.
\item[24. B] Both infer the sufficient condition from satisfaction of a necessary condition.
\item[25. D] Failure to find evidence is not, by itself, proof that the event never occurred.
\item[26. E] The principle prioritizes the mural's imminent, irreversible loss.
\end{description}

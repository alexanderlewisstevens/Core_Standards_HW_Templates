Practice reading the notation and definitions used in the Proofs Core. There are 60 short statements. Read each statement as written before choosing an answer.

  • True: a complete proposition that is true.
  • False: a complete proposition that is false.
  • Not a truth-evaluable proposition as written: incomplete, ill-formed, undefined, or containing an unbound variable. This is different from False.

Given: Bound variables range over the integers ℤ. P and Q denote propositions, and P(n) denotes a proposition about an integer n. A formula with an unbound variable is not a complete proposition.

Notation: ∀ means “for every”; ∃ means “there exists”; P → Q means “if P, then Q”; P ↔ Q requires both directions. A witness establishes an existential claim; a counterexample disproves a universal claim.

Identify what is assumed, what must be proved, and the scope of each variable. A complete but incorrect claim about a proof strategy is False, not the third option.

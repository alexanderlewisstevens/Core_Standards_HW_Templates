Practice reading the notation and definitions used in the Induction Core. There are 60 short statements. Read each statement as written before choosing an answer.

  • True: a complete proposition that is true.
  • False: a complete proposition that is false.
  • Not a truth-evaluable proposition as written: incomplete, ill-formed, undefined, or containing an unbound variable. This is different from False.

Given: P(n) is a proposition indexed by integers n ≥ 1. In an induction step, k is a fixed but arbitrary integer in the stated range. Also let a1 = 2 and an+1 = an + 3 for n ≥ 1.

Notation: P(k) is the statement at index k; P(k) → P(k+1) is the induction-step claim; an is the nth sequence term; ∑ means to add the indexed terms. Strong induction may use all earlier established cases.

Distinguish the base case, the hypothesis, and the next case. Track subscripts and the allowed range of every index before judging truth.

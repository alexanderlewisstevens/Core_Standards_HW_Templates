Practice reading the notation and definitions used in the Counting Core. There are 60 short statements. Read each statement as written before choosing an answer.

  • True: a complete proposition that is true.
  • False: a complete proposition that is false.
  • Not a truth-evaluable proposition as written: incomplete, ill-formed, undefined, or containing an unbound variable. This is different from False.

Given: X = {a,b}, Y = {b,c,d}, n = 5, and r = 2. A fair die has sample space Ω = {1,2,3,4,5,6}, with events E = {2,4,6} and F = {4,5,6}. Z is the number shown.

Notation: n! is factorial; P(n,r) counts ordered selections without replacement; C(n,r), also written “n choose r,” counts unordered selections without replacement; |X| is the size of X; Pr(E) is the probability of event E; Ec means “not E”; Pr(E ∣ F) is the probability of E given F; E[Z] is the expected value of Z.

Identify the objects being counted and whether order or replacement matters. For probability expressions, distinguish an event from a random variable.

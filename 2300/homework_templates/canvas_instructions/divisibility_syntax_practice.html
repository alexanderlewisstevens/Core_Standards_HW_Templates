Practice reading the notation and definitions used in the Divisibility Core. There are 60 short statements. Read each statement as written before choosing an answer.

  • True: a complete proposition that is true.
  • False: a complete proposition that is false.
  • Not a truth-evaluable proposition as written: incomplete, ill-formed, undefined, or containing an unbound variable. This is different from False.

Given: a, b, and c are arbitrary integers, and m ≥ 2 is a fixed integer. All other variables are integers when quantified or assigned. In a ∣ b, the left integer divides the right integer. A base-b numeral may use only digits 0,…,b − 1.

Notation: a ∣ b means “a divides b”; a ∤ b means “a does not divide b”; a ≡m b, also written a ≡ b (mod m), means a and b have the same remainder modulo m; gcd(a,b) is the greatest common divisor; (dk…d0)b is a base-b numeral.

Read the direction of divisibility carefully, identify the modulus or base, and check that every required operand is present before judging truth.

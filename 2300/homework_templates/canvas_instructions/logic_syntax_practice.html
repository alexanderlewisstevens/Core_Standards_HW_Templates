Practice reading the notation and definitions used in the Logic Core. There are 60 short statements. Read each statement as written before choosing an answer.

  • True: a complete proposition that is true.
  • False: a complete proposition that is false.
  • Not a truth-evaluable proposition as written: incomplete, ill-formed, undefined, or containing an unbound variable. This is different from False.

Given: p and r are true; q is false. Variables range over the integers ℤ. P(n) means “n is even,” Q(n) means “n > 0,” and R(a,b) means “a < b.” P and Q each take one input; R takes two.

Notation: ¬p means “not p”; p ∧ q means “p and q”; p ∨ q means “p or q” (inclusive); p → q means “if p, then q”; p ↔ q means “p exactly when q”; ∀ means “for every”; ∃ means “there exists”; ∴ means “therefore.”

Identify the main connective or relation, its inputs, and each quantifier’s scope. Check that the statement is complete before judging its truth.
